Portfolio Case Study

Conversion Rate Optimization for an E-commerce Running Shoe Brand

A simulated CRO case study focused on identifying the root cause of declining sales and developing an evidence-based recommendation.

Independent Portfolio Case Study — Simulated Business Scenario

This project was created as part of my professional development to demonstrate my approach to marketing analysis, root cause investigation, and evidence-based decision-making. It does not represent work completed for a real client.

Business Situation

An e-commerce company specializing in premium running shoes experienced a decline in sales despite relatively stable website traffic and consistent marketing performance.

Leadership wanted to understand why revenue was decreasing and determine the most effective course of action without making unnecessary changes to pricing, advertising, or other parts of the business.

Business Challenge

The primary challenge was to identify the root cause of declining sales while avoiding reactive decisions based on assumptions.

Instead of immediately increasing advertising or reducing prices, I approached the problem as an investigation and evaluated the available evidence across multiple areas of the business.

Investigation Process

  1. Reviewed key business metrics, including website traffic, conversion rate, average order value, and marketing performance.
  2. Compared current performance with previous reporting periods to identify meaningful changes.
  3. Investigated possible causes including pricing, website performance, advertising, customer behavior, inventory, and customer support trends.
  4. Eliminated unsupported assumptions by relying on the evidence available.
  5. Considered information from multiple business functions rather than treating the problem as purely a marketing issue.

Key Findings

The investigation showed that marketing campaigns, website traffic, and pricing remained relatively stable.

The primary issue was that one of the company's highest-selling products had become unavailable because of an inventory shortage.

Marketing was still attracting qualified visitors, but customers were unable to purchase the product they wanted.

Root Cause

The decline in sales was primarily an operational and inventory issue, rather than a marketing performance issue.

Recommendation

  • Immediately stop promoting the unavailable product.
  • Coordinate with Operations regarding inventory replenishment.
  • Inform Customer Support so representatives can recommend suitable alternatives.
  • Redirect marketing efforts toward products that remain available.
  • Monitor sales performance after inventory recovery before making additional marketing changes.

Expected Business Impact

  • Reduced customer frustration.
  • Lower unnecessary marketing spend.
  • Improved customer experience.
  • More effective use of available inventory.
  • Potential recovery of lost sales after inventory is restored.

What I Would Do Next

After implementing the immediate recommendations, I would monitor performance rather than assuming the problem was solved.

  • Track conversion rate and revenue before and after inventory recovery.
  • Monitor product-level performance to determine whether customers are successfully shifting toward available alternatives.
  • Review customer support questions and complaints for additional friction points.
  • Analyze whether marketing traffic converts differently across available products.
  • Reassess the situation if the expected recovery does not occur.

Skills Demonstrated

Conversion Rate Optimization · Marketing Analytics · Root Cause Analysis · Cross-Functional Collaboration · Executive Decision-Making · Data-Driven Marketing · Problem Solving

Key Takeaway

The biggest lesson from this project is that strong marketing decisions come from investigating the evidence, identifying the root cause, and making recommendations based on data rather than assumptions.
