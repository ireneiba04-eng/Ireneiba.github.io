Marketing Crisis Management

Prioritizing business problems through root cause analysis, risk evaluation, and cross-functional decision-making.

Independent Portfolio Case Study — Simulated Business Scenario

This project was created as part of my professional development to demonstrate marketing crisis analysis, problem prioritization, risk evaluation, and evidence-based decision-making. It does not represent work completed for a real client.

Business Situation

A growing e-commerce company experienced a sudden decline in marketing performance. Several campaigns were producing weaker results, customer acquisition costs were increasing, and leadership was concerned about the impact on revenue.

The marketing team had multiple possible explanations for the decline, including changes in advertising performance, customer behavior, website conversion issues, and increased competition.

Leadership needed to determine which problems required immediate attention and which could be addressed through longer-term optimization.

Business Problem

The central question

How should the marketing team prioritize competing problems during a period of declining performance while minimizing business risk?

Rather than reacting to the most visible metric, the analysis focuses on identifying the underlying causes, evaluating potential business impact, and determining which actions should be taken first.

Analysis Approach

1. Identify the symptoms

The first step is to separate observable symptoms from potential root causes.

  • Declining conversion performance
  • Increasing customer acquisition costs
  • Lower advertising efficiency
  • Reduced return on marketing investment

2. Investigate potential root causes

Potential causes are evaluated across the marketing funnel, including traffic quality, campaign performance, website conversion, customer behavior, and external market conditions.

3. Evaluate business impact

Each issue is evaluated based on its potential effect on revenue, customer acquisition, profitability, and operational stability.

4. Prioritize actions

High-impact problems with strong supporting evidence receive priority over issues that are less urgent or based primarily on assumptions.

Risk Evaluation

Crisis decision-making requires balancing speed with evidence. Acting too quickly can create unnecessary costs, while waiting too long can allow a performance problem to become more serious.

  • High risk: Problems directly affecting revenue or customer acquisition.
  • Medium risk: Performance issues that may reduce efficiency but have limited immediate financial impact.
  • Low risk: Optimization opportunities that can be addressed after urgent problems have been stabilized.

Strategic Recommendation

The recommended approach is to stabilize the highest-impact areas first, while continuing to collect evidence on secondary issues.

Marketing decisions should be based on the combination of performance data, customer behavior, financial impact, and the quality of available evidence rather than on a single metric.

Cross-functional collaboration between marketing, analytics, sales, and operations can help determine whether the problem originates within marketing or is connected to a broader business issue.

Key Takeaway

Effective marketing crisis management is not simply about reacting quickly. It requires identifying the true business problem, separating symptoms from root causes, evaluating risk, and prioritizing actions according to business impact.

This case study demonstrates my approach to structured problem solving and evidence-based strategic decision-making under uncertainty.
