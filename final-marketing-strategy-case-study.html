PORTFOLIO CAPSTONE CASE STUDY

Final Marketing Strategy

An integrated, data-driven marketing strategy connecting acquisition, conversion, retention, measurement, and long-term business growth.

Independent Portfolio Capstone — Simulated Business Scenario This project was created as part of my professional development to demonstrate integrated marketing strategy, performance analysis, customer lifecycle thinking, budget allocation, measurement, experimentation, and executive decision-making. It does not represent work completed for a real client.

Business Situation

A growing e-commerce company has successfully generated significant website traffic and customer acquisition through paid search, paid social, organic search, promotional campaigns, and lifecycle marketing.

However, growth has become increasingly difficult to manage. Marketing channels are being evaluated independently, customer acquisition costs are rising, conversion rates vary across traffic sources, and retention performance is not being incorporated consistently into acquisition decisions.

Leadership needs a unified marketing strategy that connects short-term performance with long-term customer value.

Business Objectives

The strategy is designed around five primary business objectives:

  • Improve profitable customer acquisition.
  • Increase website and landing-page conversion efficiency.
  • Improve customer retention and repeat purchase behavior.
  • Allocate marketing investment according to business impact rather than channel activity alone.
  • Establish a measurement framework that supports better executive decision-making.

Strategic Diagnosis

The primary problem is not simply insufficient traffic. The deeper issue is that marketing activity is being optimized in disconnected stages.

Acquisition may appear successful when measured through clicks, conversions, or immediate revenue, while the customers generated by those campaigns may have substantially different retention rates and lifetime values.

Therefore, the strategic priority is to move from channel-level optimization toward customer-value-based optimization.

Core strategic principle: Marketing investment should be evaluated according to its contribution to profitable customer growth, not simply the amount of traffic or short-term revenue it generates.

Customer & Funnel Analysis

The customer journey is divided into five strategic stages:

1. Awareness

Introduce the brand to qualified audiences through paid social, organic search, content, partnerships, and other discovery channels.

2. Consideration

Strengthen product understanding through relevant landing pages, educational content, social proof, product comparisons, and retargeting.

3. Conversion

Reduce friction through stronger landing-page messaging, simplified purchase experiences, trust signals, clear calls to action, and systematic CRO testing.

4. Retention

Use lifecycle communication, personalized recommendations, post-purchase education, loyalty initiatives, and re-engagement campaigns to encourage repeat purchases.

5. Advocacy

Encourage satisfied customers to generate reviews, referrals, user-generated content, and other forms of organic brand advocacy.

Audience Strategy

Rather than treating all customers as one audience, the strategy uses behavioral and lifecycle segmentation.

  • New website visitors
  • High-intent product viewers
  • First-time purchasers
  • Repeat purchasers
  • High-value customers
  • At-risk customers
  • Lapsed customers

Each segment receives different messaging, offers, and communication priorities based on its expected business value.

Channel Strategy

Paid Search

Capture existing demand by prioritizing high-intent searches, improving keyword relevance, controlling inefficient spend, and aligning landing pages with search intent.

Paid Social

Generate and develop demand through audience segmentation, creative testing, retargeting, and customer-value-informed optimization.

Organic Search

Build sustainable acquisition through content targeting relevant search intent, improving technical foundations, and developing commercially valuable organic visibility.

Email & Lifecycle Marketing

Convert and retain existing customers through welcome sequences, abandoned-cart journeys, post-purchase communication, cross-sell campaigns, reactivation, and loyalty messaging.

Content Marketing

Develop educational and decision-support content that strengthens brand authority, supports SEO, improves consideration, and assists conversion.

Marketing Investment Strategy

Budget allocation should not be based solely on historical spending. Investment should be adjusted according to performance, scalability, confidence in the data, customer quality, and strategic importance.

Efficiency Evaluate CAC, CPA, ROAS, and contribution margin.
Scale Determine whether a channel can grow without rapidly declining efficiency.
Value Evaluate retention, repeat purchases, and customer lifetime value.

The objective is therefore not to find the single channel with the highest short-term ROAS. The objective is to build an investment mix capable of producing sustainable profitable growth.

Conversion Rate Optimization

CRO will operate as a continuous experimentation program rather than a one-time website redesign.

  • Test value propositions and headline messaging.
  • Improve product-page information architecture.
  • Reduce checkout friction.
  • Strengthen trust signals and social proof.
  • Test calls to action and promotional messaging.
  • Analyze device-level conversion differences.
  • Prioritize experiments according to expected business impact.

Tests should be evaluated against meaningful business outcomes rather than selecting winners based on small changes in superficial metrics.

CRM & Retention Strategy

Retention becomes a core growth lever because increasing customer value can improve the economics of acquisition.

Lifecycle Automation

  • Welcome and onboarding sequence
  • Abandoned-cart recovery
  • Post-purchase education
  • Cross-sell and upsell campaigns
  • Repeat-purchase reminders
  • Customer win-back campaigns
  • High-value customer experiences

CRM performance should be measured through incremental revenue, repeat-purchase behavior, retention, and customer lifetime value rather than email engagement metrics alone.

Measurement & Attribution

The measurement framework connects marketing activity to business outcomes across the customer journey.

Acquisition CAC, CPA, CTR, CPC, conversion rate
Revenue ROAS, revenue, contribution margin
Customer Value Retention, repeat rate, LTV

Attribution should be interpreted carefully. Platform-reported conversions are useful for optimization but should not automatically be treated as perfectly incremental business outcomes.

Where possible, the organization should combine platform analytics, first-party customer data, controlled experiments, and broader business reporting to improve confidence in marketing decisions.

Executive KPI Framework

Leadership should receive a concise view of the metrics that matter most to business performance.

  • Customer acquisition cost
  • Customer lifetime value
  • LTV-to-CAC relationship
  • Conversion rate
  • Repeat purchase rate
  • Marketing contribution to revenue
  • Contribution margin
  • Retention performance

Operational metrics such as impressions, clicks, and engagement remain useful diagnostic indicators, but executive decisions should be tied more closely to economic outcomes.

Experimentation Roadmap

Phase 1 — Diagnose

Establish baseline performance, identify funnel bottlenecks, validate tracking, and segment customers by lifecycle and value.

Phase 2 — Test

Run controlled experiments across landing pages, creative, messaging, offers, audiences, and lifecycle campaigns.

Phase 3 — Optimize

Shift resources toward strategies that demonstrate reliable incremental impact while reducing inefficient activity.

Phase 4 — Scale

Increase investment in strategies that demonstrate sustainable performance while maintaining measurement discipline and monitoring diminishing returns.

Risk & Contingency Planning

Rising Acquisition Costs

Diversify acquisition sources, improve conversion efficiency, and increase focus on retention and customer value.

Channel Dependency

Avoid over-reliance on one platform by developing multiple acquisition and retention mechanisms.

Short-Term Performance Pressure

Maintain a balance between immediate revenue initiatives and investments that create sustainable long-term demand.

Measurement Uncertainty

Avoid overconfident conclusions from incomplete attribution data. Use multiple evidence sources and controlled testing wherever practical.

Strategic Recommendations

1. Move toward customer-value-based optimization.

Evaluate acquisition strategies using retention and lifetime value, not immediate conversion value alone.

2. Build a unified full-funnel measurement system.

Connect acquisition, conversion, retention, and customer value so leadership can evaluate marketing as one system.

3. Establish a structured experimentation program.

Prioritize tests based on expected impact, evidence quality, and business importance.

4. Treat retention as a growth strategy.

Improve lifecycle communication and repeat purchasing to increase customer value and strengthen acquisition economics.

5. Allocate budget dynamically.

Increase investment in strategies that demonstrate sustainable incremental impact while reducing or restructuring inefficient initiatives.

Expected Business Impact

The proposed strategy creates a framework for making marketing decisions based on business impact rather than isolated channel performance.

By connecting acquisition, conversion, retention, measurement, and customer value, leadership can make more informed decisions about where marketing resources should be invested.

Most importantly, the framework emphasizes sustainable growth rather than optimizing individual marketing metrics in isolation.
