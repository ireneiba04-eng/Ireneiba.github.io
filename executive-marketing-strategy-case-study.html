PORTFOLIO CASE STUDY

Executive Marketing Strategy

Translating business objectives into evidence-based marketing strategy, resource allocation, performance measurement, and executive-level recommendations.

Independent Portfolio Case Study — Simulated Business Scenario This project was created as part of my professional development to demonstrate executive marketing strategy, business analysis, strategic prioritization, performance measurement, and data-driven decision-making. It does not represent work completed for a real client.

Business Situation

A growing e-commerce company had achieved strong customer acquisition through paid search, paid social, organic search, and promotional campaigns. However, marketing activity had become increasingly fragmented.

Multiple channels were being optimized independently, while leadership lacked a unified framework for determining which marketing investments were creating the greatest business value.

Customer acquisition costs were increasing, marketing performance varied significantly by channel, and leadership needed a clearer strategy for allocating resources while protecting long-term growth.

Strategic Challenge

The central challenge was not simply increasing traffic or generating additional conversions. The business needed to determine how marketing resources should be prioritized across acquisition, conversion, retention, and customer value.

Strategic question: How should the company allocate marketing resources to maximize sustainable business growth while managing acquisition costs and maintaining customer value?

Strategic Objectives

  • Improve marketing efficiency and return on investment.
  • Reduce dependence on individual acquisition channels.
  • Increase the value generated by existing customers.
  • Establish clearer performance criteria for marketing investment.
  • Align marketing activity with broader business objectives.

Strategic Analysis

1. Business Objective Alignment

Marketing decisions should begin with the business objective rather than with individual advertising channels.

The analysis therefore considered revenue growth, profitability, customer acquisition efficiency, retention, and customer lifetime value as interconnected business outcomes.

2. Channel Evaluation

Each acquisition channel should be evaluated using both efficiency and strategic value. Metrics such as CAC, conversion rate, ROAS, revenue, and customer quality provide a stronger decision framework than traffic volume alone.

3. Customer Value

A customer who generates an initial purchase is not necessarily equivalent to a customer who returns repeatedly. Customer lifetime value therefore provides an important counterbalance to short-term acquisition metrics.

4. Risk Evaluation

Over-investment in a single channel creates concentration risk. Changes in competition, platform performance, advertising costs, or customer behavior can quickly reduce performance.

Strategic Recommendation

The recommended strategy was to shift from channel-level optimization toward a portfolio-based marketing strategy.

Rather than maximizing spending in whichever channel currently produced the highest short-term return, investment should be evaluated according to profitability, scalability, customer quality, and strategic risk.

Priority 1 — Protect High-Performing Acquisition

Maintain investment in channels demonstrating efficient customer acquisition while establishing clear thresholds for CAC, ROAS, and conversion performance.

Priority 2 — Improve Conversion Efficiency

Before significantly increasing acquisition spending, improve the website and conversion experience so additional traffic produces greater business value.

Priority 3 — Increase Customer Value

Use segmentation, lifecycle campaigns, retention initiatives, and marketing automation to increase repeat purchases and customer lifetime value.

Priority 4 — Diversify Growth

Maintain a balanced acquisition portfolio across paid, organic, and retention channels to reduce dependence on a single source of growth.

Measurement Framework

Strategic performance should be evaluated using a combination of financial, customer, channel, and operational metrics.

  • Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)
  • Return on Ad Spend (ROAS)
  • Conversion Rate
  • Customer Lifetime Value (CLV)
  • Repeat Purchase Rate
  • Revenue by Channel
  • Marketing Contribution to Revenue
  • Retention Rate

The purpose of the measurement framework is not to maximize every metric independently, but to understand how marketing performance contributes to overall business outcomes.

Implementation Approach

Phase 1 — Diagnose

Establish baseline performance by channel, customer segment, acquisition source, and lifecycle stage.

Phase 2 — Prioritize

Identify high-impact opportunities using business impact, performance evidence, scalability, and risk.

Phase 3 — Test

Run controlled experiments across acquisition, conversion, segmentation, and retention initiatives.

Phase 4 — Scale

Increase investment in strategies that demonstrate sustainable performance while reducing or restructuring initiatives that fail to meet established thresholds.

Risk & Contingency Planning

  • Rising acquisition costs: Shift investment toward higher-efficiency channels and improve conversion and retention performance.
  • Channel dependency: Develop diversified acquisition and retention strategies.
  • Short-term performance pressure: Balance immediate revenue goals with customer lifetime value.
  • Uncertain performance data: Use controlled testing and incremental investment rather than making large decisions from isolated results.

Strategic Outcome

The proposed strategy creates a structured framework for making marketing investment decisions based on business impact rather than channel activity alone.

The approach connects acquisition, conversion, retention, measurement, and customer value into a single strategic system, allowing leadership to make more informed decisions about where marketing resources should be invested.

Most importantly, the framework emphasizes sustainable growth rather than optimizing individual marketing metrics in isolation.
